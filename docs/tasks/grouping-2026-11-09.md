# Rigid groups: what to build (9 Nov)

Where we are: the model's group vectors are four-fifths right for moving
points, and the wrong fifth pulls the weighted Kabsch fit far off, so the
rigid proposal is worse than the model's own guess and the model ignores it.
With the true groups the proposal beats the guess on hidden points (0.453
against 0.380 on old Kubric). The groups are what to fix.

The idea, agreed: appearance gives a first grouping (DINOv3, later masks);
motion confirms or splits it as frames come in: points whose distances stay
fixed are one piece, the rest are not. On real video an object is not one
rigid piece (a person, a cat), so masks are a hint, motion decides.

## What others do

- RAFT-3D (Teed and Deng, CVPR 2021): rigid-motion embeddings per pixel, a
  soft grouping into rigid objects, and a differentiable Dense-SE3 layer
  (unrolled Gauss-Newton) that solves each pixel's SE(3) from the embeddings
  and correspondences. The embeddings are trained only through that layer,
  from the scene flow loss: no masks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00726
- MoSE3 (Cheng et al., Oct 2026): dense 3D point tracks plus per-pixel
  rigidity embeddings, an SE(3) at every pixel from a weighted Horn fit (a
  closed-form alignment, our Kabsch fit) between frames. The embeddings are
  trained end to end through the fit by losses on the transforms; trained on
  synthetic data only (Art-Kubric, articulated objects with SE(3) and
  rigidity labels), works on real video. https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.03716
- Segment Any Motion in Videos (Huang et al., CVPR 2025): long-range tracks
  with DINO features, attention across tracks and over time, a motion
  embedding first and semantics as support, SAM2 to turn the moving tracks
  into masks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22268
- Shape of Motion (Wang et al., ICCV 2025): every point's motion a weighted
  mix of a few shared SE(3) motion bases, a soft split of the scene into
  rigid groups. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13764
- Learning segmentation from point trajectories (Karazija et al., NeurIPS
  2024): a low-rank loss that groups tracks whose motion explains each other.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12392
- SAM 3 (Meta, Nov 2025; 3.1 in Mar 2026): segments and follows every
  instance in a video, an automatic mode beside prompts. The appearance prior
  for real video. https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam3

The lesson from RAFT-3D and MoSE3: train the grouping through the fit that
uses it. Ours is trained by its own classification loss, and the proposal is
detached, so nothing ever tells the group vectors that a wrong fifth ruins
the fit.

## Plan, one change a run

1. Object labels (the clips rendering now, `~/scratch/kubric_obj`): carry
   each point's object into the cache and train the group loss on "same
   object" instead of "distance never changes by 2 cm".
2. Through the fit: the proposal not detached, and a loss on the proposal's
   own positions against the truth (seen and hidden points), so the group
   vectors are trained by what the fit needs, as RAFT-3D and MoSE3 do. The
   group loss stays beside it.
3. An explicit motion check as input: for each pair of points, how much
   their distance has changed over the frames both are seen, given to the
   model beside appearance (an attention bias or input to the group vectors).
4. An honest backing: the fit's leftover error over the mates instead of
   how much weight they carry, so a fit that is wrong says so.
5. Later, for real video: SAM 3 masks as an appearance input, trained on its
   masks of our clips (not Kubric's true ones), as with noisy depth.

Each is judged by the group check (`scripts/check_groups.py`, now also in
every validation as group right / found), the rigid check
(`scripts/rigid_check.py`: the proposal against the guess) and hidden points
on Kubric and the ball.

## Results so far

- Object labels (full_design_object_groups, runs.md): the model's groups
  against the true objects on 60 new clips: for points moving over 64 px,
  0.894 of the mates it picks are on the point's object and it finds 0.907
  of them; slow movers (1-4 px) 0.640. The wrong mates are other objects,
  not the floor. The rigid proposal is still half as good as the guess.
- DINOv3 alone (scripts/check_appearance.py, 20 new clips): each point's
  feature at its query pixel, cosine similarity: same object against two
  objects AUC 0.80; of a moving point's most similar points, 0.773 are on
  its object (background 0.719, still objects 0.898). Raw DINOv3 is worse
  than the model's own grouping (0.894), so feeding its similarity in
  directly would not fix the groups: a stronger appearance cue (masks) or
  the motion check is what could.
- EntitySAM (scripts/entitysam_check.py, 10 new clips at 256 px, its ViT-L
  checkpoint): 22 s a 48-frame clip on an A100 (0.46 s a frame, five times
  DINOv3's caching); 7.6% of points on no mask. Points read at their first
  visible frame, put together when they share a mask id: right / found 0.737
  / 0.777 for points moving over 64 px, 0.760 / 0.896 for 32-64 px, 0.849 /
  0.688 for still points: worse than the model's own groups (0.894 / 0.907)
  and no better than raw DINOv3. On Kubric's small synthetic objects at 256
  px it merges and splits objects and loses ids through the clip; it may do
  better on real video, but it is no source of group labels or inputs here.
  The appearance route is closed for now; what is left is motion (the
  explicit check) and training the groups through the fit.

## The label, settled (9 Nov)

A group is the points that move rigidly together, judged on the frames a
window sees: two points belong together if they kept their distance there.
That holds on every dataset (all have 3D tracks) and gives body parts on
people and animals, where one object (one instance mask) is not one rigid
piece. Object labels are a Kubric shortcut, kept for measuring and as useful
knowledge ("these two are different things"), not as the loss's target.
Later, if worth it: two outputs, an object vector trained on object labels
where a clip has them, and the rigid vector trained on motion, reading it.

## More data: MOVi-F (12 Nov)

Google's released MOVi-F at 256 px (gs://kubric-public/tfds/movi_f, 1,024
training shards, 160 GB, about 9,750 clips of 24 frames; 250 validation clips)
holds depth, segmentation, object coordinates, normals, cameras and each
object's 3D box: everything our renderer uses for tracks. The first 150
training shards (about 1,400 clips) and the validation shards are converted to
our raw layout by scripts/movi_to_raw.py, the tracks and each point's object
from the renderer's own code, now shared (scripts/kubric_tracks.py). The 256 px
clips are every other pixel of the 512 px render, so each pixel's values sit a
quarter pixel in from its corner (`centre=0.25`); with that, every object
point projects back onto its query pixel (0.000 px), the tracks match the depth
(median 0.1%), and half the points move. scripts/find_duplicates.py leaves out
any clip that repeats one of the old Kubric's validation clips (TAPIP3D's
Kubric may be MOVi-F's own videos), then scripts/cache_kubric.pbs caches them
(cache/movi_f24).

Found on converting: all 840 MOVi-F clips repeat clips of TAPIP3D's Kubric
(5,991 downloaded, all of MOVi-F), 102 of them our old Kubric validation
clips: MOVi-F adds no new videos, and training on it would have tested on
training clips. What it showed instead: kubric_dense uses only 3,493 of the
5,991 downloaded clips, so ~2,500 are new data already on disk
(full_design_more_kubric). TAPIP3D's 5,991 hold some videos twice under
two names: 93 of the 2,502 uncached clips repeat a validation clip, and are
left out. kubric_dense's own 3,144 training clips repeat none of its 349
validation clips (nearest 0.264, scripts/find_duplicates.py), so the
standard scores so far are on unseen clips.
